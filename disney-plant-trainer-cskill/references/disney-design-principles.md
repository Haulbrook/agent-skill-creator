# Disney Landscape Design Principles

This document outlines the professional landscape design principles that make Disney parks' horticulture legendary. The Disney Plant Trainer skill evaluates arrangements against these principles.

## 1. Forced Perspective

**Definition**: The technique of placing taller plants in the background and progressively shorter plants toward the foreground to create an illusion of greater depth and distance.

**Implementation**:
- Back row: Tall trees and large shrubs (canopy zone)
- Middle rows: Medium shrubs and ornamental grasses (medium zone)
- Front rows: Low perennials and annuals (low zone)
- Edge: Ground covers and border plants (ground cover zone)

**Scoring Criteria**:
- Height progression from back to front
- Minimum 3 distinct height zones
- No taller plants blocking shorter ones

## 2. Color Harmony

**Definition**: Strategic use of complementary and analogous colors to create visual flow and evoke emotions.

**Color Stories**:
- **Romantic**: Pink, purple, white
- **Tropical**: Orange, red, yellow
- **Cool & Calm**: Blue, purple, silver
- **Classic**: Red, white, green

**Scoring Criteria**:
- Color diversity (minimum 3-5 hues)
- Green ratio (healthy plant mass)
- Dominant color consistency

## 3. Layering

**Definition**: Creating visual depth through multiple distinct height zones.

**Five-Layer System**:
1. **Canopy** (15+ ft): Large shade trees
2. **Tall** (6-15 ft): Small trees, large shrubs
3. **Medium** (2-6 ft): Medium shrubs
4. **Low** (6in-2ft): Perennials, small shrubs
5. **Ground Cover** (<6in): Creeping plants, mulch

**Scoring Criteria**:
- Number of distinct height zones (target: 4-5)
- Even distribution across zones
- Visual depth perception

## 4. Focal Points

**Definition**: Clear visual anchors that draw the eye and create memorable moments.

**Types**:
- Specimen trees with unique form
- Bright flowering clusters
- Topiaries and shaped hedges
- Water features surrounded by plants
- Architectural elements

**Scoring Criteria**:
- Presence of 1-2 clear focal points
- Not too many competing focal points
- Strategic placement (rule of thirds)

## 5. Symmetry Balance

**Definition**: Balanced composition without rigid formal symmetry.

**Approaches**:
- **Formal**: Mirror image on both sides
- **Informal**: Visual weight balanced, not identical
- **Asymmetrical**: Different elements with equal visual impact

**Scoring Criteria**:
- Plant count balance (left vs right)
- Height balance across axis
- Color distribution

## 6. Seasonal Interest

**Definition**: Year-round visual appeal through plant selection.

**Planning**:
- Spring: Early bulbs, flowering trees
- Summer: Annuals, perennials peak
- Fall: Foliage color, ornamental grasses
- Winter: Evergreen structure, berries

**Scoring Criteria**:
- Variety of plant types
- Evergreen backbone presence
- Seasonal bloom diversity

## 7. Maintenance Access

**Definition**: Practical spacing that allows for Disney-level maintenance.

**Spacing Guidelines**:
- Walking paths between plant masses
- Access to water sources
- Room for equipment
- Growth allowance (mature size planning)

**Scoring Criteria**:
- Spacing consistency (low coefficient of variation)
- No overcrowding
- Logical maintenance paths

## The "Disney Difference"

What sets Disney landscaping apart:

1. **Meticulous Maintenance**: Plants are replaced before showing any decline
2. **Night Lighting**: Landscapes designed to look magical after dark
3. **Themed Consistency**: Plants match the land's theme and story
4. **Hidden Infrastructure**: Irrigation and utilities completely concealed
5. **Guest Experience**: Every sightline considered for photo opportunities

## Applying These Principles

When analyzing an arrangement, the skill evaluates:

```
Disney Score = Σ (Principle_Score × Weight)

Where:
- Forced Perspective: 20%
- Color Harmony: 15%
- Layering: 20%
- Focal Points: 15%
- Symmetry Balance: 10%
- Seasonal Interest: 10%
- Maintenance Access: 10%
```

A score above 0.7 indicates professional Disney-quality design.
A score above 0.5 indicates good garden design.
A score below 0.5 suggests room for improvement.
